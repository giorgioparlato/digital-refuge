# quotes and passages for review, round 3

  

Tick what you want and I'll add it. Nothing goes in the app until you do.

  

**How to read the markers**

  

- `✓` — I read the wording in a primary or publisher source this round (linked per section).

- `~` — widely published with consistent wording, but I could not see the book text itself. Fine to use, worth a glance.

- `?` — attribution circulates but I could not confirm it. Don't ship without checking.

- **[long]** — sized for the typing challenge (`Passages.kt`), not the home screen.

- Everything else is short enough for the home screen and the lock screen.

  

**Three honest warnings before you pick**

  

1. **Rumi.** Coleman Barks, who produced most of the famous English "Rumi", does not read Persian — his books are openly "versions", not translations, and scholars show they often strip the Islamic context out. So below I separate **scholarly translations** (Nicholson, Gamard) from **Barks versions**, and label the latter honestly. If you want Barks' wording — it *is* more beautiful in English — I'd suggest crediting it as "Rumi, version by Coleman Barks".

2. **Hafiz.** Now included — but from **Gertrude Bell's 1897 translation**, which is a real translation *and* out of copyright, so there's no question about using it. What I still won't add is the hugely popular "Hafiz" of Daniel Ladinsky: those are **his own poems**, not translations, and the Persian originals don't exist. If you ever want one of those, it should be credited to Ladinsky.

3. **Nora Bateson.** Her books are not online in full, so the five below come from a Greenpeace piece quoting the book directly. They're solid, but there's less of her than of the others. If you want more, the book itself would settle it.

## Music

  

**Two things to know before picking from here.**

  

1. **Lyrics are copyrighted** in a way that Rumi and Thoreau are not, and Genius being licensed covers Genius, not us. So I check wording there, but what I put in the repo stays at a line or two with the song named — the same way a quote screen normally works. What I won't do is paste whole verses, which rules lyrics out for the **[long]** typing-challenge passages; those are better served by the public-domain and prose sources above, which have no such limit. If there's a particular verse you want, add it to the app yourself and it's your call.
