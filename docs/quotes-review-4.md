# david foster wallace, for review

Tick what you want and I'll add it. Nothing goes in the app until you do.

**How to read the markers**

- `✓` — I read the wording in a transcript or reference source this round (linked per section).
- `~` — widely published with consistent wording, but I could not see the book text itself.
- **[long]** — sized for the typing challenge (`Passages.kt`), not the home screen.
- Everything else is short enough for the home screen and the lock screen.

A note on fit: nearly all of this is from *This Is Water*, his 2005 Kenyon commencement address, which is
literally a speech about choosing what to pay attention to. It is the closest thing to a manifesto for
this app that I know of. *Infinite Jest* gives better short lines but is darker and less on-theme.

## long — *This Is Water* [long]

These are sized for the typing challenge. Line breaks become single spaces, as with the Ikkyū poems.

- [X] ✓ **the fish**
  There are these two young fish swimming along, and they happen to meet an older fish swimming the
  other way who nods at them and says, "Morning, boys. How's the water?" And the two young fish swim
  on for a bit, and then eventually one of them looks over at the other and goes, "What the hell is
  water?"

- [X] ✓ **what to pay attention to**
  Learning how to think really means learning how to exercise some control over how and what you
  think. It means being conscious and aware enough to choose what you pay attention to and to choose
  how you construct meaning from experience.

- [X] ✓ **the only real freedom**
  The really important kind of freedom involves attention and awareness and discipline, and being
  able truly to care about other people and to sacrifice for them over and over in myriad petty,
  unsexy ways every day.

- [ ] ✓ **the default setting**
  Everything in my own immediate experience supports my deep belief that I am the absolute center of
  the universe; the realist, most vivid and important person in existence.

- [ ] ~ **everybody worships** — the long run before the line you remembered. **Check this one.**
  In the day-to-day trenches of adult life, there is actually no such thing as atheism. There is no
  such thing as not worshipping. Everybody worships. The only choice we get is what to worship. And
  an outstanding reason for choosing some sort of god or spiritual-type thing to worship is that
  pretty much anything else you worship will eat you alive. If you worship money and things, if they
  are where you tap real meaning in life, then you will never have enough. Never feel you have
  enough. Worship your own body and beauty and sexual allure and you will always feel ugly, and when
  time and age start showing, you will die a million deaths before they finally grieve you. Worship
  power, you will feel weak and afraid, and you will need ever more power over others to numb you to
  your own fear. Worship your intellect, being seen as smart, and you will end up feeling stupid, a
  fraud, always on the verge of being found out.

- [ ] ~ **and they are default settings** — the sentence that closes it, good on its own or tacked on
  But the insidious thing about these forms of worship is not that they're evil or sinful; it's that
  they're unconscious. They are default settings.

## a note on the worship passage

I could not get a source to reproduce it verbatim this round — the transcript hosts return summaries
rather than the text for a block that long, which is why it's marked `~` rather than `✓` while the
one-line version above it is confirmed. What is written here is from my own reading and I believe it
is close, but a long passage is where a half-remembered clause does real damage, so give it a glance
against the transcript or the book before it ships. If you'd rather not, paste the passage here and
I'll use your text exactly, as with the Norwegian Wood ones.

Two edits I made deliberately, both easy to undo:

- I cut the list of examples after "spiritual-type thing to worship" — in the original it runs
  "be it J.C. or Allah, be it Yahweh or the Wiccan mother-goddess or the Four Noble Truths or some
  inviolable set of ethical principles". It's a lot of proper nouns to type at midnight, and it
  drags the passage somewhere this app has no business going. Say the word and it goes back in.
- I joined the dashes into commas, as the app does elsewhere.

## left out on purpose

- The "capital-T Truth is about making it to thirty, or maybe fifty, without wanting to shoot
  yourself in the head" passage. Verified and famous, but not something to meet at bedtime.
- "You will become way less concerned with what other people think of you when you realize how
  seldom they do." Constantly attributed to him, and I could not confirm it in any of his work.
  I'd leave it out rather than ship something he may not have written.
